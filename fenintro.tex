\begin{speechtext}

Okay, now the formalities are over, how do you like the new clothes?
Surprised they had a tabard in your size?
We take all sorts, `the most accepting \gls{temple} of all', and too good for nobody.

Did you never wonder how people farm the land when monster are wandering about?
Just kidding; ``wandering monsters'' would be ridiculous.
Monsters don't wander, they \emph{hunt}.
But seriously, I'm heading to the \gls{broch} at Bleatghast to deliver a letter, 
and you're heading there so the \gls{jotter} can take your details and assign you a mission.
Once we're there, I'll show you how all the farms here stay safe.
So you never heard the sound of the walls at the \gls{edge}?
You've had an easy life in your secluded, little, hole, eh?

What did you do?
Don't tell me --- I like to guess.
Did you rob houses here in town?
Maybe someone helped you through the windows at night?
No?
Don't tell me, I'll figure it out in the end.

Not tax evasion I hope?
Nah, you guys never join \glspl{temple}.
You don't even know about the gods, do you?
Well, come up to the \gls{weaversGuild} then, and I'll show you one of the gods.

See that sign with a pale man, with a black nose and black fingers?
That's `\gls{sable}'.
He collects the souls of those who die of cold.
So the \gls{templeOfFrost} has a divine duty (and exclusive rights) to sell
\ifnum\thetemperature=0%
  warm clothes to keep us toasty%
\else%
  preservatives, to keep the food good%
\fi.
All \glspl{weaversGuild} belong to the \gls{templeOfFrost}, just like us \glspl{guard} work for the \gls{templeOfBeasts}.

Check here in my bag --- two dried up, leathery, \gls{griffin} wings.
Ever seen them up close?
They look a bit like a bat's.
And in the right hands, they can make a good preservative, so I'm going to sell them here.
Should be worth at least ten \glsentrylongpl{sp} each!

I sold the rest of the \gls{griffin} yesterday, and got \pgls{coin} or two for the meat, but nothing compared to selling \pgls{basilisk}.
People can make good knives from \pgls{basilisk}'s teeth, and the fat burns for hours.

Of course we can't keep it all.
We're selling some part of \pgls{monster}, so the \gls{templeOfBeasts} takes half the sale.
That's a 50\% tax on everything you get from the forest, and remember to pay it, or you'll be back in the \gls{court}, and the next time you won't be handed a nice position in the \gls{guard}!

You can't sleep here, so let's get to the gate before dark.

Look at that pathetic beggar over there, asking for food!
He can clearly walk, but refuses to sign up with us and fight for civilization.
Even if he got eaten by something, it'll slow that something down while everyone else kills it or gets to safety.
It's a good deed.
He could be a hero.
Everyone dies a hero in the \gls{guard}.

\subsection*{\showInterval{0}~\Glsfmtname{lonelyRoad}}

\subsubsection*{\showInterval{0}~Caravans}
help you make new friends on the road.

The Sunlight's nearly with us on the road.
In a little while it'll shine right down on our heads.
Actually, I can catch a Sunbeam when I reach up right now; look!

Hey, Glugrip --- did you ever meet my friend Rudkludge?
\emph{\Gls{cutter} Rudkludge}?
I guess he never needed \pgls{notary}.
He became a real hero right here, just last \gls{cTwo}.
It was so cold his hands had frozen over, and he couldn't hold his halberd right.
I was the same way.
If \pgls{woodspy} takes you because your hands are frozen stiff, does your soul go to \gls{sable} or \gls{sylf}?

Either way, you can drop the rock now.

\subsubsection{\showInterval{1}~\Glsfmtplural{bothy}}
start as cairns, or `cenotaphs', really.
People drop a rock to remember the dead.
Once enough people drop enough rocks, there's a pile, and people ask who it's for, and those people bring more.
In the end they bring enough for a little traveller's house --- `\pgls{bothy}'.
So that's the first rock for Rudkludge, and I'll bring another next time.
One day, I'll carve his name by the door.

Before you enter \pgls{bothy}, stop and read the name by the door, so you know who's house you're entering.
And always leave it as good as you found it or better.
People who steal \pgls{bothy}'s axe should be fed to the forest.

The trees are tall here, so the long shadows come down fast, and we have four miles till the \gls{village}\ldots
hopefully we make it before Sunset, but it's not up to us.
It's up to the donkeys, and if they don't want to pull, we can't make them.
Once I'm \pgls{ranger} I'll be hard-marching everywhere, but people expect \glspl{cutter} to stay with their caravans, protecting the donkeys and helping with the steep hills.
After rests and repairs, you often travel no more than ten miles a day.

Nearly there.
Look at the open ground ahead.

Walls don't do much except keep the \glspl{basilisk} out, and give any archers a place to stand, but at least it's something.

The second layer is the farmland.
And it's all fertile out here, much better for growing than the areas around the town.
The gods curse the land when we stay too long, so the old, settled, farmlands never have the same yield as these fresh \glspl{village} by the \gls{edge}.

It was very gracious of \composeHumanName\ to bind your wounds.
She says you should lie still, and especially to not move your arms until she removes the bandages.

\subsubsection*{\showInterval{3}~The \Glsfmtplural{broch} Summon \Glsfmtplural{monster}}
with their pipes.
I said I'd tell you about `the sound of the walls at the \gls{edge}', and now we're here.

It might start with a quarry, because \glspl{broch} need a lot of stone.
\Pgls{broch} is a simple tower, with two thick layers of stone.
No windows, and just one door (or maybe two, if it's fancy).
You can sleep inside \pgls{broch} quite happily, knowing that nothing is coming in.
And you can stand at the top with \pgls{bow}, watching out for anything wandering below.

So here's the trick: bagpipes.
They're loud and cheap.
Not too cheap, but \emph{really} loud.
You can hear them five miles away, easily.
And if you play them from the top of \pgls{broch}, on top of a hill, on a calm day?
You can hear them eight miles away, sometimes ten.
And so can any nearby \glspl{monster}.

Most \glspl{monster} hunt in twilight, so every dawn and dusk, someone plays the pipes, so they get curious and approach.
Imagine a great circle of noise, emanating from each \gls{broch}; that's your safety-circle, where the \glspl{guard} pull all the dangers to a single point.
We all stand up top, \glspl{bow} at the ready, and one of us plays the pipes.

And we don't always make a kill-shot, but every arrow's an important lesson.
So the areas around \pgls{broch} become much safer, quickly.
The system's not perfect.
Things sneak past sometimes, and stop at the first \gls{village}, or find some wandering traders on the road (and traders really do `wander').
Some rare few attack the early hamlets, far from town.
But nothing gets to the centre, because it has so many tempting noises and smell on the way.

So now you know the trick, and even heard the performance from two \glspl{broch} at the same time.
It's always two here, because we're right between two \glspl{broch}, and you can hear both pipers playing.

You might have missed one set of pipes, because of your little scuffles, but we always hear two songs here in Bleatghast.
The \gls{broch}'s just up the hill, and on a clear day, you can see Wailfall \gls{broch}.
Sometimes the pipers try to play at the same time, and no two people can agree on whether or not they succeeded.

In the morning, you can tell me if they sound like they're playing together, or playing at different times.
