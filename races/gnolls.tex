\noindent
Only the gnolls have the strength and wits to live above-ground, without walls.
They keep and breed fierce hunting dogs and most keep two or three, so a group of twenty gnolls will often have around fifty.
These dogs keep watch, sometimes prowling around a camp's outskirts, sometimes simply keeping their ears up.
Nothing can sneak up on a sleeping camp.

Smaller groups hunt for their meat.
Larger groups generally herd animals, and can be heard a long way off, due to the combined noise of aurochs, goats, sheep, and gossip.

Clans are nomadic, but usually have a set rotation, wandering to a known clearing, then down a river where aurochs graze, and then off to a valley to pick herbs.
Meeting another clan in this criss-cross pattern either means a celebration, or a fight (depending on how the last meeting went).
These fights have rules, and death is rare.
Sometimes the outcome is arranged beforehand by political requirements.

Despite their clear martial strength, most clans flee when they smell \pgls{basilisk}.
While a group of twenty gnolls can certainly kill any \gls{basilisk}, it's never worth the price --- someone always dies in the fight.

\subsubsection[The Gnollish Language]{The Language}
\index{Languages!Gnolls}
\index{Languages!Dragons}
\label{gnollishLanguage}
sounds distinct from any other, due to the shape of gnolls' jaws.
Other people, with other jaws, struggle to make heads or tails of the gnolls' languages.
Tails present another problem to outsiders, as gnolls communicate with their entire body, like a super-sign language.
Spoken words tend to relate to far-away communication, such as shouting for aid, or invitations to dinner; signs signal loyalty, subtle descriptions, or veiled warnings. These two layers of communication allow fast-talking gnolls to convey a lot of information in a short amount of time.

Written word can be a strange concept for gnolls to grasp. If something is worth remembering, it's worth talking about. All knowledge being passed through rich oral traditions also encourages all gnolls in a group to bond while sharing that knowledge, as they need good relationships in order to learn something new. A gnoll would be perplexed as to why would anyone make a social activity into a solitary one, while also leaving knowledge readily available for strangers they'll never meet.

The Gnollish language shares a great deal of vocabulary with the standard speech of dragons.
According to legend, the gnoll hero Kshonk taught the dragons how to speak so that she could outwit them.

\subsubsection{The Structure}
of small groups is informally matriarchal, as female gnolls tend to be slightly stronger, faster, and louder.

When a whole tribe needs to make a serious decision about where to travel, or whether or not to fight, the decision emerges through hyperactive chatter from every member.
Every gnoll speaks at once, to all sitting beside them, in a short, hurried fashion, acknowledging and expanding upon others' points, and noting what they have heard about what others have said.
This process goes on for anywhere for twenty minutes to a full night and day.
By the end, the group always reaches a consensus.
Outsiders never understand the process (or simply can't keep up).

While all this works fine for gnolls, others quickly become irritated with their out-of-place habits.
Gnolls may assume women are in charge, assume that a man is a woman (because he's taller than the others), and they don't understand that when leaders talk, others should stop talking.

\subsubsection{Gossip}
is vital to gnolls' social life, and the way they approach problem-solving. If a gnoll has a problem, a question, or an idea, they will share it with their close group, and not ponder much over it. The group will share it among themselves, and anyone else they trust, everyone contributing their insight, until all opinions circulate back to the gnoll that started it, who will then use all the information to make a decision. Individual gnolls may not seem smart to outsiders, but that is only because they're used to offloading their thoughts to the group.

The more information a gnoll can convey, the better their social standing. Usually, the fastest and loudest gnolls will assert themselves to lead the conversation, but will quiet down and listen if someone has something interesting to say. This leads more cunning gnolls to embelish their gossip a bit, to make themselves heard, but this is done carefully and sparingly, as being labeled a liar can ostracize them.

This can be frustrating for other races, as gnolls will seem brash and impatient, and if you ever confide in them, they will share it with all their friends. Gnolls, on the other hand, not understanding why others take so long to come up with a conclusion, will quickly run out of patience and take action.

\subsection{Commerce}

In a world where travel is dangerous, nomadic gnolls make for natural traders. They trade meat and wild plants for cultivated foods, fabrics, and metalwork goods. This trade is very beneficial for both sides, but can be challenging to conduct due to gnolls' language, and understanding of currency and property.

Hunting dogs also fetch a high price, and sellers always make sure that the buyer promises to look after the animal properly.
The buyer should keep this promise, as gnolls take note of how buyers care for their animals, and may take the animal back if it's not properly cared for.
Humans sometimes complain they don't really understand `property', and the gnolls generally don't sell to those humans.

Gnolls often accept payment in weapons (blacksmithing smells horrible, so it's better to have someone without a nose do the work), jewellery (easier to carry than \glspl{coin}), and food.
Coin is rarely accepted, as it only has any value when traded for something else, but is nothing but burden while travelling.
They may also purchase tents throughout \gls{cTwo}, but discard them after.
Constant travel means carrying very little.

In addition to \glspl{ingredient} pulled from animals, every pack remembers a few spots on their regular travels where valuable plants grow.
\Glspl{bedshroom}, \glspl{marchingMushroom}, seekmist, and screechmoss, all provide something to trade (assuming the pack's \glspl{witch} don't put them to use first to make \glspl{boon}).

\subsection{Enlistment}

Gnolls have no `criminals' within their own society (every crime has a fast ultimatum, and possible redemption or death).
They enlist in the \gls{templeOfBeasts} after human society corrupts them with a taste for smoked meat, biscuits, and \glspl{coin}.
Being faster and fitter than humans, they stand a better chance at survival.

\subsection{Roleplaying Gnolls}
\index{Gnolls!Roleplaying}

The world is dangerous, and there is precious little time to act. Don't spend too much time thinking alone, it should be a group activity, and should be done quckly. Many heads think better then one, but only if they think fast.

Yours, and everyone else's ideas should be discussed openly. If people are repeating themselves, or they're being indecisive, it's time to make a decision and act. Hesitation is the most dangerous trap to fall into.
