Only the gnolls have the strength and wits to live above-ground, without walls.
They keep and breed fierce hunting dogs and most keep two or three, so a group of twenty gnolls will often have around fifty.
These dogs keep watch, sometimes prowling around a camp's outskirts, sometimes simply keeping their ears up.
Nothing can sneak up on a sleeping camp.

Smaller groups hunt for their meat.
Larger groups generally herd animals, and can be heard a long way off, due to the combined noise of aurochs, goats, sheep, and gossip.

Clans are nomadic, but usually have a set rotation, wandering to a known clearing, then down a river where aurochs graze, and then off to a valley to pick herbs.
Meeting another clan in this criss-cross pattern either means a celebration, or a fight (depending on how the last meeting went).
These fights have rules, and death is rare.
Sometimes the outcome is arranged beforehand by political requirements.

Despite their clear martial strength, most clans flee when they smell \pgls{basilisk}.
While a group of twenty gnolls can certainly kill any \gls{basilisk}, it's never worth the price --- someone always dies in the fight.

\subsubsection{The Language}
\index{Languages!Gnolls}
\index{Languages!Dragons}
sounds distinct from any other, due to the shape of gnolls' jaws.
Other people, with other jaws, struggle to make heads or tails of the gnolls' languages.
Tails present another problem to outsiders, as gnolls communicate with their entire body, like a super-sign language.
Spoken words tend to relate to far-away communication, such as shouting for aid, or invitations to dinner; signs signal loyalty, subtle descriptions, or veiled warnings.

The Gnollish language shares a great deal of vocabulary with the standard speech of dragons.
According to legend, the gnoll hero Kshonk taught the dragons how to speak so that she could outwit them.

When a whole tribe needs to make a serious decision about where to travel, or whether or not to fight, the decision emerges through hyperactive chatter from every member.
Every gnoll speaks at once, to all sitting beside them, in a short, hurried fashion, acknowledging and expanding upon others' points, and noting what they have heard about what others have said.
This process goes on for anywhere for twenty minutes to a full night and day.

By the end, the group always reaches a consensus.
Outsiders never understand the process (or simply can't keep up).

\subsection{Commerce}

When a hunt goes well, the excess meat can buy a pack some \rations, \rations, and other rich foods gnolls don't make for themselves.
Too much trade can be dangerous, as a pack reliant on trade can find themselves vulnerable to negotiations, which waste everyone's time and energy.

Hunting dogs also fetch a high price, and always make sure that the buyer promises to look after the animal properly.
The buyer should keep this promise, as gnolls take note of how buyers care for their animals.
Humans sometimes complain they don't really understand `property', and the gnolls generally don't sell to those humans.

Gnolls sometimes take coinage, but prefer jewellery, as it can be worn, and does not require additional preparation, like money-sacks.

Gnolls accept payment in weapons (blacksmithing smells horrible, so it's better to have someone without a nose do the work), jewellery (easier to carry than \glspl{coin}), and food.
They may also purchase tents throughout \gls{cTwo}, but discard them after.
Constant travel means carrying very little.

In addition to \glspl{ingredient} pulled from animals, every pack remembers a few spots on their regular travels where valuable plants grow.
\Glspl{bedshroom}, \glspl{marchingMushroom}, Seekmist, and Screechmoss, all provide something to trade (assuming the pack's \glspl{witch} don't put them to use first to make \glspl{boon}).

\subsection{Warfare}

Gnolls almost universally employ guerilla tactics.
They set settlements on fire, attack supply lines, and generally poke at every weakness which comes from living in a fixed location.
This works well against people who live above-ground, but not against anyone who lives below-ground, where making fires just makes you cough, and getting lost in the \gls{deep} becomes a real danger.

\subsection{Inheritance}

\label{gnollishInheritance}
mean that Gnolls, like wolves, can grab and damage in a single attack by sinking their teeth into a target.
This deals $1D6$ Damage as usual, plus the Strength Bonus.

\subsubsection{Air \Glsfmtplural{ingredient}}
\index[mana]{Air!Gnolls}
can be created from a gnoll's intestines.
Gnoll \glspl{witch} typically extract intestines from the dead.
Others refer to this as a `death ritual', but in fact, gnolls simply value practicality, and rarely bother with rituals.

\subsection{Enlistment}

Gnolls have no `criminals' within their own society (every crime has a fast ultimatum, and possible redemption or death).
They enlist in the \gls{templeOfBeasts} after human society corrupts them with a taste for smoked meat, biscuits, and \glspl{coin}.
Being faster and fitter than humans, they stand a better chance at survival.
